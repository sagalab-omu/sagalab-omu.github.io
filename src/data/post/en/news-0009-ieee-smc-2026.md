## Our papers have been accepted at IEEE SMC 2026

Papers by **Issa Osawa** and colleagues from our laboratory, and by alumnus **Takuya Tamada** and colleagues, have been accepted at the **IEEE International Conference on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics (IEEE SMC 2026)**, organized by the IEEE Systems, Man, and Cybernetics Society.

IEEE SMC 2026 will be held in Bellevue, Washington, USA, from October 4 to 7, 2026. The presentations will take place at the conference.

### Overview

#### Dynamic causal structure analysis of physical fatigue with dynamic Bayesian networks

Preventing occupational accidents in physically demanding industries such as construction requires continuous monitoring of workers' physical condition. Research on predicting health status with wearable devices and machine learning is advancing, but much of it relies on black-box models that make it hard to explain the underlying physiological mechanisms. Physical fatigue is also not a static state but a dynamic process that accumulates over time.

This study proposes a new method that uses **Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBN)** to uncover the temporal causal structure linking wearable sensor data (heart rate, physical activity, sleep quality, and more) with subjective fatigue. By clustering daily longitudinal data according to workload intensity (such as %HRR), the DBN models captured how the physiological transitions and carryover effects of fatigue change dynamically with the work context.

The results provide an interpretable framework for building preventive safety management and personalized alert systems in real-world settings.

#### Probabilistic matrix factorization with multimodal embedding fusion

**Probabilistic Matrix Factorization (PMF)**, a representative recommender system technique, estimates latent preferences from users' rating histories. Its accuracy tends to drop when rating data are scarce, so researchers have explored using auxiliary information such as item descriptions, images, and connections between users.

Our laboratory has been developing methods that incorporate auxiliary information such as text, images, and social networks into PMF. Building on that work, this study proposes a multimodal recommendation method that fuses embeddings obtained from three sources, **text, images, and social networks**, and integrates them into PMF.
